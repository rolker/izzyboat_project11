## Summary

Successful OTH operation that mostly held together — `udp_bridge`
improvements paid off, annunciator feedback came through cleaner with
the small-wins PRs in, and real progress on getting segmentation into
the costmap and on sorting out the position-source mess from the last
deployment. Still want an "OTH mode" that quiets the WiFi WARN cascade
when we're intentionally past WiFi range. The bad part of the day was
a collision: autonomy was re-engaged before the boat had fully cleared
a floating platform, and the cross-track-error correction pulled it
right back into the obstacle. No serious damage to the boat, none to
the platform — recovery worked by taking Ruby (our support RHIB) out
to BizzyBoat and then escorting BizzyBoat back to the pier while
piloting it with the RC controller — so the takeaway is a concrete
learning experience rather than a setback.

## Lessons Learned

- **Give way more room before re-engaging autonomy.** Past, not beside; the
  cross-track-error correction is unforgiving.

- **Camera feeds by themselves make situational awareness more challenging.**
  An experienced operator can work cameras-only; a costmap displayed in CAMP
  would have been a game-changer for tight-quarters work, but it doesn't
  substitute for the operator's own awareness practice.

- **Control lag at OTH range makes using manual control challenging.** Today's
  mistake was driven by wanting out of laggy manual sooner than the situation
  demanded.

- **Bench-side prep cuts can bite in the field — but excessive prep costs water
  time, and the balance is hard.** Today the multi-instance segfault would have
  been caught by Phase 4 prep we skipped, and the cart-side Phase 1 pass didn't
  translate to a working in-water trackline. The lesson is the tension itself,
  not a rule about always running every prep step — and I haven't fully learned
  where the right line is yet.
