## Lessons Learned

- **Three of the five CAMP deaths fall inside the window the operator describes as untroubled**
  (11:33, 12:47, 14:19). Respawn masked them completely — there was no symptom to report, and the
  deaths were reconstructed after the fact from rosout mtimes rather than observed. Any monitoring
  that depends on the operator noticing will not catch this failure.
- **Consecutive survey days are charge-limited by the schedule, not by the pack.** The day starts with
  less margin than the voltage curve alone suggests, and that margin is invisible until it runs out
  in the afternoon.
- The battery watch produced a false alarm within a minute of arming (a methodology error, corrected
  and re-baselined at 07:35) and later a real one that survived sample-by-sample checking. A watch
  that cries wolf once costs credibility on the alert that matters.
- Hourly point-projections of time-to-threshold were repeatedly revised earlier, then later, then
  earlier again as the discharge rate changed. The agent's own 14:01 reframing away from
  point-projections toward a band was the more useful form.
- Shipping code to a running survey stack worked here — three fixes, all deployed, all in the binary
  that flew the day — but every one was operator-directed, and the destructive step was gated on an
  independent check that the stack was down.
