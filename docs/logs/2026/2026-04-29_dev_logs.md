**2026-04-29T09:03-04:00** — **Mercat serial port: resolved.** The agent
working the mercat thread posted a root-cause + permanent-fix write-up
on #76 ([comment](https://github.com/rolker/unh_echoboats_project11/issues/76#issuecomment-4343858780),
posted 08:57 EDT). Recurring "SBG COM4 locked after reboot" mechanism
identified: at boot, Windows' legacy `sermouse` driver loads and
`serenum.sys` attaches as a per-device upper filter on
`ACPI\PNP0501\SMODULEC4`, opens COM4 exclusively to PnP-probe for a
serial mouse, and collides with the SBG's live binary stream — port
stays wedged below user-mode (`Status: OK` in PnP, exclusive-open denied
to userland). Permanent fix on mercat: `Set-Service sermouse -StartupType
Disabled`. Verified post-reboot: `mode COM4` returns full port settings,
`.NET SerialPort.Open()` returns `IsOpen=True` with 2826 bytes inbound
after 200 ms (SBG actively streaming). PnP disable/enable cycle on the
device instance retained as a fallback if a different probe culprit
appears in the future. Diagnostic fingerprint captured in the comment
for future recognition.

**2026-04-30T13:45-04:00** — **RDP-link quality vs operator's relative bearing
from boat** (revised after re-analysis with world-frame + fixed-position
heading test). Tests the perception that RDP dropouts cluster at
certain relative bearings.

**Initial framing was wrong about the boat antenna.** The boat-side
WiFi bridge radio is the **MikroTik OmniTIK 5 ac** (per
`docs/bizzyboat_network.md` — "factory-provided", mounted on the
factory WiFi mast / camera-tower centerline at x≈+0.31 per
`bizzyboat_reference_geometry.md`), which is **omni in azimuth** with
~8 dBi gain. The directional element is the **SXTsq Lite5** on shore
(~60° beam, fixed pointing). So the original "boat antenna's main
effective direction is roughly toward the port quarter" reading was
the opposite of how the link is configured. Corrected analysis below.

Method: per-sample we compute three quantities for each diagnostic
sample (range-stratified to >50 m):

- **World-frame bearing FROM shore TO boat** (where the boat is, in
  shore antenna's frame) — tests the SXTsq directional pattern.
- **Relative bearing of shore FROM boat body frame** — tests
  body-frame asymmetry of the boat antenna; should be flat for a
  true omni.
- **Range** from shore to boat.

Then a fixed-position heading test: bin boat positions into 30 m
spatial cells, keep cells with ≥100 samples and ≥3 distinct heading
buckets occupied, and look at SNR variation across heading WITHIN
each cell. If SNR varies with heading at a fixed position, the cause
is body-frame (boat-side); if SNR is flat within a cell but varies
across cells, the cause is world-frame (shore-side).

### Result C — fixed-position heading test (smoking gun)

13 spatial cells qualified (≥100 samples, ≥3 heading buckets each,
≥20 samples per bucket). Of those:

- **11 of 13 cells (85%) show ≥6 dB SNR variation across heading**
  WITHIN the cell.
- **Median delta across qualifying cells: 10 dB.**
- **p95 delta: 23 dB. Max: 23 dB** (cell at +30 m E, 0 m N — same
  world location, SNR 30 dB at one heading vs 53 dB at another).

That conclusively shows the asymmetry is **boat-side body-frame**,
not just shore-side world-pattern: at a fixed position, only the boat
heading is changing, and SNR moves by ~10–20 dB. The shore antenna's
gain is constant for that position, so the variation has to be on the
boat side.

### Reading

The boat antenna **is** an omni in datasheet terms, but the practical
pattern is far from omni once mounted in its boat environment. The
OmniTIK is on the centerline-mast / camera tower (x≈+0.31, surrounded
by a metal mast cage with uprights at x∈{+0.30, –0.40} and y=±0.18,
plus the camera tower itself extending to z=1.41). 5 GHz wavelengths
(~6 cm) interact strongly with metal obstructions at sub-metre
distances. The result is a body-frame pattern with **10–20 dB
shadowing variation** across azimuth, even though the antenna element
itself has no preferred direction.

The aggregate body-frame asymmetry (best 210–240°, worst 330–360°)
in Result A is the *average* outcome — but the cell-level results
show the best/worst direction varies from cell to cell, indicating
the body-frame pattern is more complex than a single null. There's
also a measurable shore-side world-frame contribution in Result B.
**Both effects coexist; the boat-side body-frame asymmetry dominates.**

### Practical implications

1. **Repositioning the OmniTIK** (away from the mast cage / camera
   tower, or onto a mast extension above the cage) is a real option
   if 10–20 dB of recoverable SNR matters for class-day reliability.
   That requires a hardware change, not a config tweak.
2. **Survey-line orientation can favor good headings** if you accept
   the body-frame pattern as it stands. The aggregate-best body-frame
   bearing for the operator is ~210° (operator on port quarter) —
   plan legs so that's roughly where the operator station lives
   relative to the boat heading. Caveat: cell-level results show
   the optimum direction varies across the harbor, so this is a
   rough heuristic, not a hard rule.
3. **Shore-side SXTsq aiming review**: the 9.1% → 28% ping-loss
   spread by world bearing suggests the SXTsq's main beam isn't
   perfectly aligned with the boat's typical operating area, or the
   beam is narrower than the area. Worth verifying its azimuth /
   tilt next deployment.
4. **Bandwidth-tuned RDP** (from the bandwidth analysis entry) helps
   regardless — at 4× lower bandwidth the link tolerates the
   shadowed bearings better.
