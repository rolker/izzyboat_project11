## Summary

BizzyBoat ran a dockside session plus a short Cod Rock shakedown out of the Judd
Gregg pier on 2026-08-24, to exercise a day of CAMP and nav2 fixes on the water
before the Isles of Shoals survey. **The fix the deployment existed to validate
held.** `bond_timeout` 8.0 was applied on gabby and verified live, and the nav2
stack never bounced — no `Deactivating`, no `Destroying bond`, no
`compute_path_through_poses` failure anywhere in the session, across 42 minutes
covering a full survey pattern, an AIS avoidance manoeuvre, and later four
consecutive real planner timeouts at the pier. The operator-station checks passed
too: CAMP memory stayed flat at ~1.12 GB against a history of ~6 GB steady and a
15.7 GB OOM kill, and the helm admission floor recorded zero admission-gate drops
on both uplinks all session.

Two problems surfaced that were not on the card. Survey lines were marked
`done: true` without the boat flying them — a known defect, tracked as
unh_marine_navigation#104 — which means mission status cannot distinguish a
skipped line from a completed one. And the operator lost the RGB camera feeds
during close-quarters docking and hand-drove the boat alongside on the
segmentation feed plus CAMP position. The dropout was subsequently measured
rather than inferred: inbound throughput collapsed to 2–10 % of normal across
17:29–17:31 while the boat kept transmitting normally, against a Starlink
`pop_ping_drop_rate` spike of 19 % at 17:29 and roughly 1.29 million resend
give-ups over the session.

## Lessons Learned

- **A parameter that reads back correctly proves nothing about whether it was
  applied.** This bit twice. `bond_timeout` could not be set from any YAML because
  the lifecycle manager never reads a params file — it takes inline `parameters=`
  only, so a YAML entry would have been silently inert. And on 2026-08-25 the same
  class of trap cost a three-minute bridge outage. The correct check is
  behavioural, never the readback.

- **Mission status cannot be trusted as a coverage record.** Skipped lines are
  marked done, so a mission reads 100 % complete with holes in it. Coverage has to
  be judged from the live coverage layer or the recorded bathymetry. Over the
  horizon this distinction is operationally load-bearing.

- **Send counters measure handoff, not delivery** (udp_bridge#72), and this
  session produced a clean field reproduction: the bridge reported ~140 kB/s tx
  into a cell modem with **no SIM card fitted**. Judge a link by
  `received_bytes_per_second` or `/diagnostics`, never by send counters.

- **Seeding a host log makes its own repo skip every `make sync`** for the
  duration of the deployment, because the untracked file reads as a dirty tree.
  Harmless here, but on 2026-08-24 gabby pushed 8 commits to `jazzy` mid-session,
  so the skip *can* hide real incoming changes on a busier day.

- **AIS was steering the boat with none of the driving data recorded.** `ais_layer`
  went live 2026-08-20; no AIS topic was in any record list until this session
  added them. The successful AIS avoidance manoeuvre on 2026-08-24 could not have
  been reconstructed from a bag. Fixed in `bizzyboat.yaml` and verified loading.

- **`TopicStatistics` has no per-topic receive counter**, so per-topic delivery at
  the operator end cannot be reconstructed from a bag at all. This is why the RGB
  outage had to be pinned from per-connection rates and boat position instead.

- **Deriving a window from independent data beats asking the operator to confirm
  a hypothesis.** The field agent first asked whether the RGB loss matched a
  give-up peak it had itself proposed, got hedged agreement, and correctly flagged
  the answer as led. The docking window was then recomputed from `/tf` boat
  position alone and landed on the same three minutes — which is what made the
  correlation real.
